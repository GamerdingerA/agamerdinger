Nordic public AI funding register

Part of the Algorithms, Data and Democracy (ADD) project · Aalborg University

Governments around the world are directing more public funds towards artificial intelligence (AI). Whether the money goes to research, concrete AI solutions, computing infrastructure or organizational capacity, each investment has its own purpose.

Governments are open about their funding decisions, but the decisions are rarely added up or followed over time. This register does both for four of the Nordic countries, Denmark, Finland, Norway and Sweden.

The register is associated with the research project Infrastructuring the Public Sector's use of AI (IPA), which is part of the ADD project.

It is compiled and regularly updated by Alexander Gamerdinger and Torben Elgaard Jensen. We plan to widen its scope in the near future.

Methodology

The register follows the coding rules of the IPA Nordics article.

What is counted

Public money National public money, decided by parliament, government, ministries, state agencies, research councils or state-owned funds. Private and philanthropic money is excluded, and mixed projects enter with their public share.
AI as the aim A decision enters when it names AI as its object or aim and states an amount. General digitalization budgets and tax measures are excluded, as are defense and police packages where the AI share cannot be separated.
Firms Business programs, loans, guarantees and equity for firms are recorded but kept out of the figures. Some national strategies count them as AI investment.
EU money EU grants count only where a country co-finances them. For shared facilities such as LUMI and the EU AI factories, each country enters its own contribution.
Sources and updates Every row links to its source, a primary one whenever it exists. New decisions are searched for every week and enter only after one of the authors has approved them.

Dates and totals

Funding date Money is placed in the spending years the source states and spread evenly over them. Money from the budget for 2027 belongs to 2027, even when the bill was presented in 2026. Without stated years, the budget year is used.
Funding to date All spending years up to and including 2026.
All recorded funding Funding to date plus the future years of multi-year decisions, announced and proposed money included.
Status The stage records how far a decision has come, from announced to adopted by parliament or the deciding body. Withdrawn money stays in the data but is not counted. The phase shows where today falls within the spending years.

Amounts

Exchange rates Amounts are nominal and converted to euro at one fixed rate per currency for all years (1 DKK = €0.1338, 1 NOK = €0.0853, 1 SEK = €0.0911). Changes over time therefore come from the amounts alone. Amounts given as "up to" enter at the ceiling.
Per inhabitant Funding divided by population in 2024, in millions (Denmark 5.97, Finland 5.61, Norway 5.55, Sweden 10.6).

Purposes

The purposes follow the IPA Nordics article, with its applied AI development split into AI solutions and computing infrastructure.

Research Basic and fundamental research, including money for academic staff and doctoral training.
AI solutions Money to develop or roll out concrete AI applications.
Computing infrastructure Supercomputers and AI factories, including the training of national language models.
Organization Investment in organizational capacity. This covers national units that orchestrate AI, such as task forces and coordination units, as well as national AI hubs and research institutes.
